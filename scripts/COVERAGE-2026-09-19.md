# Beat coverage — what the recording actually demonstrates

Built from `RECORDING-SCRIPT.md` as it stands on 2026-09-19. One row per beat:
what a viewer *sees*, and which claim that sight *proves*. Then the inverse —
everything `RECORDING-READINESS.md` asserts that **no beat shows**.

The inverse list is the point. A readiness doc lists what is true; a script
lists what is demonstrated; the gap between them is what the recording is
asking to be taken on trust.

### Gap 1 is the one that matters

Every severance beat demonstrates **retention and staleness** — the tier keeps
*serving* what it had, and the parent *labels* what it no longer gets. None
demonstrates the tier **reaching a new conclusion while cut off**, which is
the stronger claim the architecture actually makes and the one §E 1.2 records
(*"region keeps computing severity, `asset_logistics_status` 1–3s old"*).

A viewer watching BEAT 3 or BEAT 5 sees timestamps advancing. Advancing
timestamps prove the *pipeline* runs. They do not show a **decision** — a
state transition, an alert, a severity change — being made locally with the
parent unreachable. That would be a tactical event appearing on the edge
screen *during* the cut and **not** at HQ until heal, which is a far stronger
image than an age counter and is currently demonstrated by nothing.

**This is why no beat depends on a live event, and why the emit-path question
does not block the recording** — the script never asks the feed to produce
anything. It is also exactly why the gap is worth closing later: the feature
most worth showing is the one the script quietly routes around.

### Consequence for the emit path

Because gap 1 and gap 2 are both unshown, **no beat in the current script
requires a tactical event to be emitted during the recording.** An empty feed
is narratable (§F) and no beat's success depends on it. A round-trip probe on
a baselined asset to prove the emit path live was therefore **not run** — its
precondition was that some beat needed a live event, and none does.

If a future cut of the script adds a local-decision beat, that changes: the
emit path would move from *nice to have verified* to *load-bearing*, and it
would need proving on a baselined asset before the recording rather than after.
